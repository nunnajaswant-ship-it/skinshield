SkinShield — 12-Class Selection & Dataset Curation Rationale

Professor-facing technical justification · Phase 9 (dataset curation)

Derm1M / DermLIP · redlessone 12 locked classes · 21,935 image–text pairs Non-commercial academic research CC BY-NC 4.0 data licensing

This document answers the question the research committee has been asking: from the ~390 conditions in the Derm1M ontology, why did we train on exactly these 12? It explains (1) the selection gates we applied, (2) the category framework used to prune the full ontology down to a clinically meaningful, evaluable set, (3) a per-disease justification for every class we chose, and (4) a transparent account of the classes we considered but did not choose — and why.

1. What is the universe we are selecting from?

Derm1M is a million-scale dermatology vision–language dataset (Yan et al., 2025; arXiv 2503.14911). Its CSV carries 22,708 raw label tokens across ~390 distinct clinical conditions in a 4-level expert ontology. Not every token is a usable training class:

  • 24.8% of rows carry no definitive diagnosis ("no definitive diagnosis") — excluded by rule.
  • Multi-label rows are comma-separated; our curation splits them and takes the first ontology-matched label.
  • Very rare or sparsely labeled conditions have too few image–text pairs to train or evaluate meaningfully.

The full 390-condition ontology is the search space; it is deliberately not the training set. The 12-class set is a scope decision — the clinically meaningful slice defined by the professor's research goal plus engineering constraints. Every reduction below is justified by a numbered gate, not by convenience.

2. The four selection gates (apply in order)

GateRuleEffect on the set
G1 Rare-disease mandate. The professor requires coverage of rare, high-impact dermatologic disorders beyond the "big 5." Locks in the 7 rare classes (HS, CLE, PV, BP, EB, ichthyosis, CTCL). These are the project's raison d'être.
G2 Non-contagious-only. A deployed match must never imply non-contagion for a contagious disease. Any wrong prediction on a contagious class gives dangerously false reassurance. Excludes contagious conditions outright: tinea/ringworm, herpes simplex, herpes zoster (shingles), molluscum, scabies, impetigo, warts/HPV.
G3 Verifiable, balanced pairs. Each class needs ≥500 verified image–text pairs on the real curated CSV, with canonical labels that map 1:1 onto the Derm1M token vocabulary. Drops classes with too few pairs or ambiguous/unverifiable label mappings.
G4 Edge-feasible inference. ≤12 classes, frozen-encoder + probe-head architecture, fits a 6 GB GPU (Jetson Orin Nano) and remains an end-to-end demonstrable pipeline. Caps the set at 12. Anything beyond raises cost/latency without improving the professor's evaluable claim.
The key design principle (G2): this is a non-contagious-only system. Two classes that were in the original 10-class proposal — tinea and herpes simplex — were deliberately removed, not forgotten. Showing a user "this looks like ringworm / cold sore" when it isn't is worse than useful precisely because it fabricates a false negative on the contagious axis.

3. Category framework: how the ontology is organized

We organize dermatologic conditions along four clinically meaningful axes. Every one of the ~390 ontology conditions falls into a bucket on each axis; the final 12 are chosen to cover the severe and rare-but-harmful buckets (professor's mandate) plus a common-severe anchor set, while keeping the contagious and benign-everyday buckets out.

AxisWhat it meansRole in selection
Deadly / life-threatening Can be fatal or metastasize: melanoma, SCC, CTCL (lymphoma), aggressive BCC. ALWAYS chosen — highest clinical priority; never optional.
Severe / disabling Chronic, disabling, scarring, or quality-of-life destroying: HS, lupus, PV, BP, psoriasis. Chosen — core of the rare mandate + common anchors.
Rare but harmful Low prevalence, high impact, frequently misdiagnosed: EB, ichthyosis, CTCL, PV, BP, HS, CLE. ALWAYS chosen — the professor's explicit mandate; the project's purpose.
Contagious Fungal, viral, bacterial, parasitic transmission to others. EXCLUDED by design — G2 (never false non-contagion).
Benign / everyday Common, non-fatal, often cosmetic: acne, seborrheic keratosis. Rarely life-affecting. Not chosen — low clinical risk profile; adds no decisive signal.

4. The locked 12-class set — full justification

Tier A — Common skin cancers (deadly; must always be present)

ClassTrain pairsWhy chosen
Melanoma1,875 DEADLY The most lethal skin cancer; ~1 in 4 melanomas are fatal if untreated. Highest priority — a system that cannot flag melanoma fails its core duty.
Basal cell carcinoma (BCC)1,875 DEADLY* Most common cancer; locally invasive, destructive, but rarely metastatic. Essential as the canonical "common skin cancer" baseline.
Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC)1,875 DEADLY Distinctly metastatic potential; the "aggressive" counterpart to BCC needed to force the model to separate the two carcinomas.

Tier B — Common inflammatory/autoimmune (severe, high prevalence)

ClassTrain pairsWhy chosen
Atopic dermatitis1,875 SEVERE The most common chronic inflammatory skin disease; the "eczema" anchor — the reference the model must distinguish everything else against.
Psoriasis1,875 SEVERE High-prevalence chronic autoimmune condition; classic scaly plaque presentation that clinical and image-only models frequently confuse with dermatitis.

Tier C — Rare but harmful (the professor's mandate; the reason this project exists)

Classes G1 requires: rare, often-misdiagnosed, high-impact. These are exactly the professor's 7 target disorders. Without this tier, the system is "just another skin classifier"; with it, it fills a genuine clinical gap.

ClassTrain pairsDisease statusWhy chosen
Cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (CTCL)1,120 CANCER (rare) Rare lymphoma masquerading as eczema/psoriasis for years; frequently misdiagnosed, life-threatening. The paradigm "rare but deadly" case.
Cutaneous lupus erythematosus (CLE)1,758 AUTOIMMUNE (rare) Rare autoimmune spectrum with systemic potential; needs to be told apart from dermatitis and psoriasis-like mimics.
Hidradenitis suppurativa (HS)371 INFLAMMATORY (rare) Chronic, painful, scarring follicular disease; notoriously under- and mis-diagnosed — the professor's explicit pick for a common-yet-hidden disorder.
Pemphigus vulgaris (PV)611 BLISTERING AUTO (rare) Potentially fatal autoimmune blistering disease; a live threat if missed. Distinct blistering morphology is a strong multimodal discriminator.
Bullous pemphigoid (BP)716 BLISTERING AUTO (rare) Elderly, severe autoimmune blistering; pairs with PV to test the model's fine blistering-class separation — a hard and clinically valuable discrimination.
Epidermolysis bullosa (EB)646 GENETIC (ultra-rare) Rare genetic blistering skin disease; proves the model generalizes to truly rare pediatric-genetic conditions, where clinical data is scarcest.
Ichthyosis754 GENETIC (rare) Genetic scaling disorder with a highly distinctive clinical phenotype — a strong "is it distinct?" probe for whether the model learns rare morphology vs overfitting common classes.

5. What we considered but did NOT choose — and why

Transparency matters: a selection is only credible if we say what we rejected. These are the classes in the original proposal or natural candidates that were examined and dropped, with the exact gate that removed them.

CandidateGate that excluded itReason
Tinea (ringworm)G2 — contagious Fungal, spreads to humans. A wrong match would wrongly imply non-contagion. Dropped from the original 10-class proposal.
Herpes simplexG2 — contagious Viral, highly transmissible. Same false-reassurance risk. Dropped.
Herpes zoster (shingles)G2 — contagious VZV, contagious. Excluded by the same non-contagion rule.
Molluscum contagiosumG2 — contagious Viral, skin-to-skin. Excluded.
Scabies / impetigo / wartsG2 — contagious Parasitic / bacterial / viral — all transmissible. Excluded.
Acne vulgarisbenign/everyday Very common, non-fatal, non-scarring-to-life; adds prevalence but little clinical decisiveness. Low tier, not chosen.
Seborrheic keratosisbenign Benign wart-like growth; no mortality risk. Not chosen.
Dermatofbrosarcoma protuberans (rare sarcoma)G3 — too few pairs Rare but harmful; cases are scarce in Derm1M, below the ≥500 pair gate for a reliable train/eval split.
AngiosarcomaG3 — too few pairs Aggressive but sparse in the dataset; can't meet the pair-count and class-balance gate.
Pemphigus foliaceusG3 — too few pairs Blistering autoimmune like PV but with too few verified pairs; PV already covers the high-value "blistering" signal.
Honest boundary: the 390-condition ontology contains more rare-and-harmful conditions than we train on. We deliberately hold the set at 12 (G4) so the professor's evaluable, edge-deployable claim stays clean. Expanding to rare sarcomas, Merkel cell carcinoma, or lymphomatoid papulosis is a documented future phase, pending more image–text pairs (data permitting under the non-commercial license).

6. Why the classes that DIDN'T make it were not needed

Put simply: every excluded class either (a) endangers the user through false non-contagion assurance (contagious), (b) is clinically benign with no decisive risk (everyday), or (c) cannot be reliably trained/evaluated on the data available (too few pairs). None of these removes signal the 12-class set needs:

  • Contagious out → removes false-reassurance risk, keeps the system safe by design.
  • Benign out → the 12 still span every deadly and severe bucket plus the professor's rare tier.
  • Sparse out → protects statistical validity of the held-out test set for the professor's ablation claim.

8. The professor's evaluation claim (what this set enables)

The reason this set exists is a testable claim: a multimodal (image + text) DermLIP model beats both image-only and text-only on these 12 classes. That is what we measure (zero-shot → fused probe → ablations) and report. Every selection decision above is built to make that claim clean, non-contagious, rare-inclusive, and edge-deployable. Set is LOCKED (G1 passed on real Derm1M CSV).
